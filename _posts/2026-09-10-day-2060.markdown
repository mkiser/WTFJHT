1/ **The Supreme Court blocked Missouri from using its Republican-drawn congressional map in the November midterms**. The unsigned order leaves the state to use its 2022 map for the midterms, which keeps intact a Kansas City-area seat that Democrats currently hold. The ruling also suspended a federal judge’s order that revived the GOP-favored map after Missouri’s Supreme Court ruled the new districts couldn’t take effect unless voters approved them in a referendum. Election officials, however, must now run the general election using different district lines than those used in the August primary. 2/ **A federal appeals court refused to let the Trump administration enforce new restrictions on mail-in ballots before the midterms**, leaving in place an injunction blocking the U.S. Postal Service rule. The rule would require states to provide the Postal Service with voter information and unique ballot barcodes and would allow postal officials to return or refuse delivery of ballots that don’t comply. The three-judge panel concluded that the rule is likely unlawful because the Constitution gives states and Congress – not the executive branch – authority to regulate elections, and warned that implementing it now would “likely result in the disenfranchisement of millions of voters” while providing “minimal – if any – gains in combating voter fraud.” ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us-appeals-court-rejects-trump-bid-implement-mail-in-voting-restrictions-2026-09-10/) / [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/trump-mail-voting-executive-order-midterms-3e2c89ab0a0f7faca75e50ff34adee15) / [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-10/supreme-court-weighs-us-mail-ballot-fight-as-doj-losses-mount) / [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/appeals-court-trump-usps-mail-ballots-first-circuit/) / [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/sep/10/usps-mail-ballot-voting-court-ruling))

4/ **The Trump administration plans to send $500 rebate checks to nearly 1 million Affordable Care Act enrollees in 30 states weeks before the November elections**. The payments will go to people who bought coverage through HealthCare.gov without premium subsidies using surplus fees collected from insurers to operate the federal marketplace. The White House claimed without evidence that the Biden administration had “overcharged.” Health policy experts, however, said some of the surplus dates back to Trump’s first term. The administration didn't explain how the $500 payment was calculated, and it's unclear whether congressional approval is required. Despite issuing dozens of subpoenas, no charges have been brought, and it's unclear what evidence even supported the conspiracy theory.
