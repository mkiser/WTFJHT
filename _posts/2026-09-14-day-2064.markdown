3/ **The EPA repealed Biden-era greenhouse gas limits on coal- and gas-fired power plants** and proposed a separate rule to prevent future administrations from restoring them. The EPA claimed that power-plant greenhouse gases don't “significantly contribute” to dangerous pollution and therefore don't meet the Clean Air Act threshold for regulation. Power plants, however, are the nation’s second-largest source of carbon dioxide emissions. The EPA estimates that revoking the standards would save the energy industry more than $300 billion. The Biden administration had estimated that its rule would produce $370 billion in climate and public-health benefits. ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-13/trump-epa-to-rescind-carbon-standards-for-coal-gas-power-plants) / [New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/13/climate/epa-power-plant-climate-rules.html) / [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/994112/ai-data-center-pollution-health-epa) / [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/14/epa-power-plant-carbon-emissions-trump-coal-climate) / [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/climate-environment/2026/09/14/epa-plans-scrap-biden-era-rules-that-limit-power-plant-climate-emissions/) / [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/epa-power-plants-trump-coal-gas-climate-cac1c2c75f8656d8eaf5f0a240edae15) / [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-epa-power-plants-climate-pollution/))

6/ **A second federal judge blocked Trump's attempt to restrict mail-in voting for the midterm election**. U.S. District Judge Carl Nichols, a Trump appointee, found that "no statute grants the Postal Service the power" to refuse to deliver mail-in ballots in states that don't upload voter lists or use envelope barcodes. Nichols warned that allowing the rule could leave "a significant number of absentee or mail-in ballots will ultimately not be counted." The Supreme Court, meanwhile, is considering the Trump administration’s request to lift a separate injunction issued by a federal judge in Boston. ([New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/14/us/politics/trump-mail-ballot-nichols-supreme-court.html) / [Democracy Docket](https://www.democracydocket.com/news-alerts/second-judge-halts-trumps-election-power-grab-barring-states-from-voluntarily-working-on-mail-voting-rule/) / [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/03/nx-s1-5955614/trump-mail-in-voting-ruling-court-usps) / [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/trumps-mail-in-voting-restrictions-blocked-by-second-judge-2026-09-14/) / [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-election/second-federal-judge-freezes-trump-mail-ballot-order-supreme-court-rcna597633))

7/ **New York and 21 other states sued the Trump administration to block its new “public charge” rule**, which would give immigration officials wider discretion to deny green cards and visas based on applicants’ use of public benefits. The states argued that the Department of Homeland Security exceeded its authority because Congress never changed the longstanding definition of who is considered a “public charge.” The rule is set to take effect Friday. DHS estimates that more than 950,000 people could disenroll from or decline to enroll in public benefits as a result. ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/14/us/immigration-public-charge-green-card-lawsuits) / [New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/14/us/politics/green-cards-immigration-lawsuit.html) / [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/14/trump-immigration-benefits-lawsuit-mamdani-nyc) / [Politico](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/09/14/new-york-city-leads-lawsuit-against-trumps-public-charge-immigration-rule-01074188) / [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/states-cities-sue-block-trump-immigration-rule-public-benefits-2026-09-14/) / [Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/us-news/law/mamdani-sues-trump-administration-over-new-immigration-rule-8ab66b4f) / [ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Politics/mayors-states-sue-trump-new-green-card-restrictions/story?id=136426646))

8/ **The Department of Homeland Security’s inspector general found that Florida’s now-closed “Alligator Alcatraz” detention center confined immigrants in small metal cages**. At least 79 detainees spent nearly two hours in small enclosures that staff described as “calming areas.” Investigators called the use of cages “highly unconventional” and posed “significant risks” to detainees’ health and safety. At least one enclosure may have been used as punishment. The watchdog also found detainees lived in cramped, windowless units, lacked daily access to showers, and encountered bug-infested facilities.
