1/ **Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth announced a new, four-star "AUTOWARCOM" to develop and deploy drones and other autonomous weapons**. The Pentagon, however, will need legislation to establish the new command, which Hegseth wants operational by October 2027. Hegseth also ordered a 20% reduction in positions for generals and admirals by Jan. 1, 2027, doubling a cut he ordered last year. He called it "accountability and long overdue," claiming “We are no longer the woke department or the weak department. Simple translation of that: No fatties, no trannies, no beardos, no weirdos, no wimps, no radicals. Just warriors.” Sen. Mark Kelly warned that Hegseth was "purging a generation of great leaders of the military" and that it would take a generation to recover. Meanwhile, Hegseth tapped Elon Musk, Anduril co-founder Palmer Luckey, and former House Speaker Newt Gingrich for a 120-day study on the future of warfare. Musk's and Luckey's companies both benefit from federal defense contracting and have a financial stake in how the military modernizes itself. The White House moved the money that Congress had allocated for border surveillance and drug-trafficking enforcement into a Homeland Security account for "commemorative events." Federal law that prohibits the use of public funds for “propaganda purposes.” FCC Chair Brendan Carr nevertheless called the ads "normal" public service announcements. 3/ **The Supreme Court agreed to decide whether ICE can detain immigrants who crossed the border illegally years ago without a bond hearing**. The Trump administration changed its interpretation of a 1996 law last year to mandate detention during deportation proceedings, even for people who have lived in the U.S. for decades. The policy reversed nearly 30 years of practice that allowed them to seek release while their cases proceeded. Nine of the 11 federal appeals courts have rejected the administration's interpretation. The justices are expected to rule by the end of June. ([New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/01/us/politics/supreme-court-trump-migrant-detention.html) / [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/supreme-court/supreme-court-decide-lawfulness-trumps-immigrant-detention-policy-rcna600251) / [Politico](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/10/01/supreme-court-trump-ice-detention-01103024) / [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us-supreme-court-hear-clash-over-trumps-immigration-detention-policy-2026-10-01/))
