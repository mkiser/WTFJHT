1/ **Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton privately blamed the Iran war and high fuel prices for his struggling Senate campaign**. "It's the war, it's gas prices, it's diesel," he told donors and lobbyists at a closed-door fundraiser, calling affordability "by far our biggest problem." Paxton also complained that Washington was controlling his campaign messaging. "I've never been in a race where I felt less control over the campaign and, like, what issues are going to matter because it's all coming – you know – from D.C.," Paxton said, adding, "I don't control message or what we're doing." While Paxton claimed that Trump's midterm convention in Dallas "dropped our numbers," Trump nevertheless is making his third trip to Texas in less than a month to campaign for Paxton. Polls show Democrat James Talarico narrowly ahead despite Republicans spending more than $200 million on the race. Trump won the state by more than 13 points in 2024. 4/ **Trump's tariffs added an estimated 2.9 percentage points to consumer goods inflation**. Without the tariffs, goods prices would have fallen slightly The estimate reflects the 12 months ending in February 2026, when the inflationary effect peaked. Nearly 90% of Trump's 2025 tariffs passed through to U.S. import prices almost immediately. Roughly a third of the increase in consumer goods prices came from American-made goods as manufacturers paid more for imported materials and raised their own prices. 8/ **The World Health Organization couldn't assess the risk of a possible plague outbreak after a Russian anti-plague institute researcher died of pneumonia of unknown origin**. WHO officials don't know what caused her illness, but Russia denied any accident at the lab and maintained that "there are no epidemic risks," despite quarantining or monitoring about 200 of her contacts. The WHO is also seeking clarification about unconfirmed reports that a second lab employee developing pneumonia of unknown cause. The U.S., meanwhile, sent Moscow a formal diplomatic warning over its failure to disclose details about the death. Secretary of State Marco Rubio described the information Russia had shared as "still quite limited," adding: "Maybe there's nothing there. Maybe it's bigger than we thought. We don't know." Trump, however, maintained that the U.S. didn't believe the incident involved a bioweapon: "We're going to find out soon enough, but we don't think so." He acknowledged that "Russia is not saying much," but repeated Moscow's assurances that the situation was under control. Trump also claimed he had a call scheduled with Putin to discuss the incident. Kremlin, however, denied that a call had been arranged.
