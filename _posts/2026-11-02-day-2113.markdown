2/ **Democratic Rep. Haley Stevens endorsed Republican Mike Rogers over Democratic Senate nominee Abdul El-Sayed days before the midterms** in a race that could decide control of the Senate. Stevens had promised to support El-Sayed, who defeated her in the Democratic primary, as recently as Saturday night. But on Sunday, however, she attended a Detroit church service with Rogers instead of a planned unity event with El-Sayed, blaming her decision on being told to stay away. "I don't belong in Abdul's campaign," she said after reportedly being asked to not attend the unity event because the dispute had become "way too distracting." The feud dates to the primary, when El-Sayed criticized pro-Israel groups for spending millions to support Stevens. She accused him and his allies of antisemitic rhetoric, and later demanded that El-Sayed denounce an outside group's ad blaming U.S. aid to Israel for high grocery prices, which she called antisemitic. El-Sayed, however, had already rejected the ad. "At the end of the day I actually don't think about Haley Stevens," El-Sayed said, adding later: "None of this matters." 4/ **A federal judge dismissed the Trump administration's effort to delay certification of Alaska's Senate election if Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan lost to Democrat Mary Peltola**. The Justice Department wanted the state to set aside the ballots cast for another candidate named Dan Sullivan and investigate whether voters had intended to support the incumbent. U.S. District Judge Sharon Gleason, however, ruled that the government offered "no workable plan" to investigate voters without violating their right to a secret ballot. A Democratic operative connected to Mary Peltola's campaign and national Democrats reportedly recruited the second Sullivan to potentially draw votes away from the Republican incumbent. Peltola has denied any involvement. 5/ **The Supreme Court rejected Trump's last-minute attempt to set aside thousands of California mail-in ballots**. The administration claimed that more than 20,000 people on California's voter rolls were likely noncitizens and wanted to separate their ballots until their citizenship could be verified. California officials, however, said the list is filled with errors and warned that separating the ballots could disenfranchise eligible voters. Researchers found that about 42% of the names on the list aren't on the state's current voter rolls and that some were naturalized U.S. citizens. The Supreme Court issued its unsigned decision hours after the Justice Department filed its emergency appeal, offering no explanation and noting no dissents. ([Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/midterm-elections-immigration-voter-fraud-trump-48998968a23a4af2f59eaea3dba4d2f3) / [New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/02/us/politics/trump-supreme-court-california-ballots.html) / [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/11/02/supreme-court-denies-trump-administration-request-set-aside-calif-ballots/) / [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/02/politics/trump-supreme-court-california-noncitizen-ballot) / [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/trump-administration-takes-california-ballot-fight-supreme-court-2026-11-02/) / [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-02/trump-s-doj-rejected-by-supreme-court-on-california-ballots) / [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/02/nx-s1-6023802/supreme-court-california-voting-ballots-trump))

6/ **Trump spent about half of his final speech before the midterms promoting his Washington renovation projects at the newly reopened Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool**. He held up pictures of plumbing materials, promoted the new White House helipad, and discussed his proposed triumphal arch. He suggested renaming the pool the "Trump Reflecting Pool" before calling it a joke. Trump also repeated his claim that vandals had damaged the pool. His own Justice Department, however, had determined that a faulty installation had caused the damage. When Trump finally turned to the election, he called it "the most important midterm, I believe, in our lifetime," accused Democrats of being "a party that hates America," and acknowledged that the president's party usually "doesn't win the midterms." Republicans, he nevertheless claimed, "gave this a very strong shot."
