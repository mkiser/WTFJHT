1/ **Trump said he “would not mind” seeing Jensen Huang reinstated as Nvidia CEO after his investment in the company’s stock grew to as much as $62 million before Huang resigned**. Trump's financial disclosures suggest he held between $13 million and $62 million in Nvidia shares at the end of August, up from $615,000 to $1.3 million at the end of 2024. Trump suggested that Huang had “learned a big lesson.” Nvidia’s board, however, is not considering bringing Huang back. Huang resigned last week after acknowledging an affair with a former executive at Nvidia customer Megaspeed, who U.S. officials suspect of spying for China, as well as other alleged affairs with Nvidia employees. 2/ **The Commerce Department issued new restrictions to prevent Chinese companies from accessing advanced U.S. AI chips through overseas data centers**. The new rule requires licenses to ship advanced chips to data centers in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, and Brazil. The Commerce Department said the rule was "written by and at the direction of the Department of Treasury and the National Security Council," after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent blamed Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick for resisting tighter export controls. Bessent said the Treasury and three other agencies had pushed for months to blacklist Megaspeed, the Singapore-based Nvidia customer under investigation for allegedly diverting chips to China. During a Situation Room meeting last week, Bessent reportedly confronted Lutnick about why Megaspeed hadn't been added to the trade blacklist, which would've restricted its access to U.S. technology. Lutnick replied: “I don’t know. I’ll look into it.” Trump, meanwhile, defended Lutnick, saying he had no plans to fire him, and suggested that the delay was intended to avoid a "blow-up with China" ahead of his meeting with Xi Jinping this week. "One wants to be friendly with China, and one wants to have a blow up with China," Trump said. "They're fine." 5/ **Trump refused to take responsibility for Friday's nationwide FAA ground stop**. Trump remained silent for nearly two days about the four-hour ground stop that disrupted thousands of flights and affected hundreds of thousands of passengers, posting on social media roughly two dozen times about unrelated subjects, including Hollywood legislation and his White House renovations. When Trump finally addressed the ground stop, he blamed the Biden administration for making an "old and broken" air traffic control system worse, lashed out at an NBC News reporter who questioned his response, and demanded more money from Congress. "I don't take any responsibility," Trump said, insisting that he'd been working from the Oval Office during the emergency and left public briefings to Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin. When NBC News correspondent Yamiche Alcindor asked whether he had prioritized White House renovations over the nation's air traffic system, Trump called her "pathetic," referred to "losers like you," and told her, "You don't have talent. You don't know what a broken column is." He insisted that the renovations took only "10 minutes a day." ### ⏰ The Dept. of WTF Just Happened.

*A few updates from Saturday’s **Last Week in WTF**, in case you missed them.*

1. **The FAA lifted a nearly five-hour nationwide ground stop Friday after an air traffic control outage**. A contractor doing routine maintenance to upgrade a copper communications wire to fiber optic cable in a remote facility caused a router to overload the air traffic control system with more than 1,000 connection attempts every four to five seconds. “This is an emergency,” Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said, warning that without significant upgrades the air traffic control system was “going to continue to fail." He added, “Give us the money to fix it.” Duffy had been seeking $30 billion from Congress to upgrade the system, but then upped his demand to: "I want $50 billion." Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin, meanwhile, said there was “zero evidence” that a cyberattack or artificial intelligence played a role. He said technology upgrades were “overwhelming” the existing flight communication system. ([Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/ground-stop-flights-airlines-cancellations-faa-1fbf4992f0ddaa4c463b0e4990b6e291) / [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/thousands-us-flights-canceled-delayed-air-travel-system-recovers-shutdown-2026-11-14/) / [New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/live/2026/11/14/us/faa-nationwide-ground-stop-flights) / [Politico](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/11/13/faa-orders-nationwide-grounding-of-passenger-planes-01166043) / [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/live-blog/live-updates-faa-lifts-ground-stops-airports-rcna609302))

Kreiss-Tomkins is the youngest governor in state history. To regain elimination status, the U.S. must stop continuous transmission for at least 12 months.
