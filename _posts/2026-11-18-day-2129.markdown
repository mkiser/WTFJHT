1/ **House Democrats nominated Hakeem Jeffries for speaker, putting him on track to become the first Black speaker of the House**. He won the party’s vote 251-11. The full House, however, must still elect him on Jan. 3, when Democrats will have one of the biggest incoming majorities in decades. “It’s a ‘good trouble’ moment in America,” Jeffries said after accepting the nomination. Meanwhile, a group of seven newly elected democratic socialist lawmakers said they voted against Jeffries because "our constituents sent us to Congress with a mandate" to deliver change on "Medicare for All, stopping ICE terror, getting money out of politics, [and] ending the genocide in Gaza." When asked about the defectors, Jeffries replied: “The results of today’s elections speak for themselves. 2/ **Democrat Mary Peltola flipped Alaska’s Senate seat, defeating Republican incumbent Dan S. Sullivan and independent Dan J. Sullivan**. Peltola will be the first Alaska Native to serve in the Senate, calling the victory “a monumental step forward.” Her win gives Democrats a 56-44 Senate majority after flipping nine Republican seats in the midterms. Robert Garcia, who is expected to chair the House Oversight Committee, sent 12 letters to Trump Jr., Eric Trump, Jared Kushner, JD Vance, White House chief of staff Stephen Miller, Attorney General Todd Blanche, and others. The planned investigations include alleged conflicts of interest involving Trump family businesses, misconduct by immigration officials, the administration’s handling of the Jeffrey Epstein files, and Ghislaine Maxwell’s transfer to a minimum-security prison. 4/ **House Democrats opened an investigation into whether Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick delayed restrictions on Nvidia chip exports to benefit his sons' investment firm**. Democrats demanded records from Cantor Fitzgerald, which Lutnick previously ran and his sons now control, concerning its Nvidia holdings and communications about chip export controls with the Commerce Department. Lutnick, meanwhile, reportedly resisted placing restrictions on Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia customer suspected of diverting advanced chips to China. The firm's Nvidia holdings were valued at about $1.2 billion when Lutnick took office. 6/ **Trump arrived in China for the first-ever three-way meeting between the leaders of the U.S., China, and Russia**. Xi personally greeted Trump at the airport, a rare diplomatic gesture that included a handshake and hug at the foot of Air Force One. Putin, however, received no such greeting when he arrived. Trump is scheduled to meet with Xi and Putin to discuss nuclear arms reductions and the wars in Ukraine and Iran. China, however, has resisted nuclear arms negotiations, arguing that the U.S. and Russia first need to reduce their much larger arsenals, while Ukraine has urged the leaders to not make decisions about the war without Kyiv. Ahead of the meeting, Xi called for "free and open trade," while Putin argued Western sanctions were "incapable of halting growth – let alone crushing the economies of competitors." Separately, Trump has planned talks with Putin, their first formal meeting since a summit in Alaska last year failed to produce a ceasefire in Ukraine.
